Body Mapping & Embodied Practice

My trauma-informed practice recognises that complex stress lives in both the mind and the body. By moving beyond traditional talk therapy, we utilise embodied practices like body mapping—tracing an outline of the body to create a life-sized map representing aspects of your unique lived experiences—safely allowing your story to be visually shared and understood.

Trauma Focused Therapy

Trauma can deeply disrupt your sense of safety, leaving your mind and body stuck in a constant state of survival. Together, we work at a pace that feels safe for you to gently process these overwhelming past experiences, helping you resolve the root causes of distress so you can reconnect with yourself and move forward.

Schema therapy

When we experience complex stress or trauma early in life, we often develop deep, rigid patterns of thinking and feeling—called 'schemas'—to help us cope. While these patterns protected us back then, they can leave us feeling stuck in repetitive loops of anxiety, self-doubt, or relationship difficulties as adults. Schema therapy goes beneath surface-level symptom management. Together, we work to identify these underlying patterns, understand their origins, and heal the root emotional wounds so you can break free from old cycles and build a healthier, more fulfilling life.

Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT)

When emotions feel overwhelmingly intense, it can be incredibly difficult to find your footing or navigate daily life. Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT) is a practical, skills-based approach designed to help you regain your balance. The word 'dialectical' simply means balancing two opposite truths: accepting yourself exactly as you are right now, while actively working to change the patterns that cause you pain. Together, we focus on building practical tools for mindfulness, emotional regulation, and distress tolerance, empowering you to navigate life’s challenges safely and with greater confidence.

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) is a foundational, evidence-based approach that helps us look at the practical connections between our thoughts, feelings, and behaviours. While standard CBT is excellent for identifying immediate habits and learning day-to-day symptom management, I view it as an entry point rather than the final destination. In our sessions, we utilize these practical behavioural skills as a stable baseline, allowing us to safely step deeper into holistic frameworks—like schema therapy and body mapping—to resolve the underlying roots of complex trauma.
